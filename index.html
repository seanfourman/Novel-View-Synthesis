the challenge

Why is this hard?

01
Depth
A 2D photo loses the third dimension. We have to guess what's near and what's far.
02
Occlusion
Parts of the scene are hidden behind other parts. From a new angle they should appear.
03
Lighting
Shiny and reflective surfaces look completely different from each angle.
04
Consistency
All the new views need to agree with each other and with the originals.
before NeRF

Classical approaches

Mesh + texture
+ free movement, measurable
− geometry/texture artifacts
− no view-dependent effects
Photo tour
+ photo-real at capture points
− very limited movement
− visible crossfades
2020 · breakthrough

NeRF

Neural Radiance Fields

Represent the whole scene as a tiny neural network. Ask it about any 3D point — get color and density. Render any view.

Mildenhall et al., ECCV 2020

how a pixel is born

Cast a ray.
Sample along it.
Add it up.

For every pixel of the output image, shoot a ray from the camera into the scene. Sample points along it, ask the network for color and density at each, blend them together.

honest assessment

Limitations

🐢
Slow training
Hours to days per scene on a GPU.
📸
Many photos needed
Dozens to hundreds, with known camera poses.
🌫️
Static scenes only
Movement, wind, people — break the model.
🌆
Hard at scale
A whole city is much harder than a single object.
the field today

What came after

Instant-NGP
2022 · NVIDIA
Hash-grid encoding. NeRF training in seconds, not hours.
Mip-NeRF
2021 · Google
Anti-aliasing across scales — sharp from any zoom level.
Gaussian Splatting
2023 · INRIA
Replace the network with millions of fuzzy 3D points. Real-time. State of the art.
takeaway

Novel View Synthesis

  • Learn a scene from photos.
  • Represent it as a function: (x,y,z,dir) → (color, density).
  • Render any new camera by casting rays and adding samples.
  • Result: a 3D world you can fly through.
